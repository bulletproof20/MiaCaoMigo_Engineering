# Ana Lourenço

Ana Lourenço carries the **adoption and loss** thread for the launch simulation: one cat joins her household from shelter intake, another senior cat reaches the end of life during the same May window, and a future wellness visit remains on the calendar into June.

## Who she is in the ecosystem

Ana registered on **6 May 2026**, the same day as Gonçalo Rego’s account and ahead of Marta Ribeiro’s desk registration on **8 May**. She is an active client with Portuguese portal preferences and a household that mixes **joyful intake** with **bereavement documentation**.

Her animals in the operational cast are **Pipoca**, adopted after shelter reception, and **Bento**, described as a senior cat whose status moves to deceased within the simulation.

## How her story unfolds

On **5 May**, Ana meets Pipoca through shelter photos while Pipoca arrives as an internal transfer from Patinhas. On **6 May**, Isabel helps book Pipoca’s **12 June** general check; Ivo and Isabel share the onboarding moment. Ana adopts Pipoca on **8 May** — the same day as Gonçalo’s Rogerim adoption — with Tiago completing the adoption and Navarro selling a starter kit.

Bento’s arc is clinical and emotional rather than commercial. João Marcelo’s care for Bento leads to a clinical exit; on **28 May**, Bento is recorded as **deceased** after decline (euthanasia in the operational narrative). Tiago also appears in recording that status change.

Pipoca’s **12 June** general visit remains **scheduled** with Marcelo — booked when Ana became a client — placing Ana in the same “future appointment” family as Gonçalo’s Jonas dermatology date.

Ana does not appear in the return, overdue invoice, or authentication-typo clusters. Her narrative weight is **continuity after loss**: the clinic still serves the surviving cat and keeps a future visit on the books.

## Relationships that matter

Tiago Mendes connects Pipoca to shelter intake and adoption completion. João Marcelo is the clinician for both Bento’s final chapter and Pipoca’s ongoing care. João Navarro may appear at the margins of retail for other households but is not the centre of Ana’s documented friction. Isabel and Ivo sit in the usual desk and administrator roles for registration and coordination.

Ana’s story validates adoption from internal shelter stock, deceased animal status, and scheduled future care in one household — a different shape from Gonçalo’s two-pet busy calendar or Marta’s commercial disputes.
